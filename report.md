Q1. The noise rate on the 45,000 training images is 40.24%. Most noise comes from visually similar pairs, and it flows both ways: truck and automobile (1,247 trucks labelled automobile, 1,126 the reverse), cat and dog (924 and 834), deer labelled horse (683) or dog (491), and airplane labelled ship (379). These pairs share shape, texture or background, so annotators confuse them. The noise is therefore class-dependent and structured, not uniformly random.

Q2. Memorization starts around epoch 37. Until then, accuracy on mislabeled training images stays near 14% (mean of epochs 1 to 35), because the network fits true classes, not wrong labels. After epoch 37 it climbs steadily: 25% at epoch 45, 37% at epoch 50, 71% at epoch 60. Meanwhile noisy validation accuracy peaks at 0.551 (epoch 37) then falls to 0.494, and clean validation accuracy falls from 0.77 to 0.69. Wrong labels are being fitted, which hurts generalization. I stopped at epoch 37, chosen by noisy validation accuracy only.

Q3. Cleaned retraining (Run 5) was the best single signal: precision@k 0.881, AUROC 0.963, versus 0.869 and 0.958 for plain cross entropy. Rank averaging all five runs gave the best AUROC (0.965) at similar precision (0.880). Failure mode: human noise sits on ambiguous images, so hard but correctly labelled images (for example cat versus dog) look like wrong labels. About 12% of the top k flagged images are actually correct. SOP made this worse, because it also absorbs hard correct images and produced more false alarms than plain cross entropy.

Q4. Relabeling won on noisy validation (0.581, versus 0.579 for ELR and 0.494 for the cross-entropy baseline; the gap to ELR is within noise). It helped because it fixes the data, not the loss. Averaging the five Task 3 models kept 28,185 labels, changed 11,786 to the predicted class and dropped 5,029 uncertain images, raising training-label accuracy from 60% to 90%. With mostly correct labels there is little to memorize, so validation accuracy kept rising to epoch 60 instead of peaking near epoch 37 and falling. Clean validation accuracy reached 0.856, versus 0.686 for the baseline.

Q5. The ranking did not change: relabeling beat the baseline on both, by 8.7 points on human noise (0.494 to 0.581) and 9.9 points on synthetic noise (0.427 to 0.525). Synthetic noise was easier to clean: one baseline model raised label accuracy from 60% to 94%, versus 90% with five models on human noise. Uniform noise scatters wrong labels, so they contradict what the network learns early. Human noise concentrates on confusable pairs (cat and dog, truck and automobile) where the model itself is unsure. On synthetic labels, relabeling used one model's predictions, since the Task 3 models saw human labels.
